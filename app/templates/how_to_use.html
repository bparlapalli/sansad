How to use ParamaSrota

A searchable record of what was actually said on the floor of the Lok Sabha

ParamaSrota parses Lok Sabha debate PDFs into individual, attributed statements — who said what, on which date, on which subject — instead of leaving them buried in a scanned transcript. Below are a few things people actually come here to do, and exactly how to do them.

"I want to see what happened in Parliament on a specific day"

1. Go to Search and click the By Date tab.

2. Pick the sitting date from the dropdown.

3. Hit Search — every statement from that day appears in order, with who said it and what kind of statement it was (question, answer, speech, ruling).

Shortcut: the Today page does this automatically for the most recent sitting day, plus an AI-written summary of what happened.

"I want to know what the PM (or my MP) has been saying lately"

1. Go to Search and click the Politician tab.

2. Start typing their name — a dropdown of matching MPs (with how many statements each has) appears as you type.

3. Hit Search — every statement from them shows up, most recent first.

4. Once you have results, a "Refine with free text" box appears above them — type a topic (e.g. "budget", "farmers", "railways") to narrow it down to just what they've said about that specific subject.

"I want to see how what an MP said has changed over time — or find something they said a while back"

1. Same starting point as above — Search → Politician tab, search their name.

2. Every one of their statements is dated, and results are newest-first — scroll down to go further back in time.

3. Use the "Refine with free text" box to jump straight to every time they've spoken about a specific bill, scheme, or issue, regardless of date — the fastest way to compare what someone said about the same subject at two different points in time.

Heads up: "back in time" is only as far back as what's currently in the dataset — see below.

"I have a keyword — a scheme, a bill, a phrase — and want to know who's said what about it"

1. Go to Search and click the Free Text tab.

2. Type the word or phrase and hit Search.

3. Each result shows the exact passage that matched — not the whole speech it came from — so you're not hunting through a wall of text. Click "Show full statement" under any result to expand it to the complete remarks.

What's in the data right now

This is an early, actively-growing dataset. It currently covers recent sitting days rather than the full parliamentary record — older sessions are being added over time. If a date or debate you're looking for isn't here yet, it's likely still in the pipeline.

Other pages

News — a running briefing of recent parliamentary activity.

PDFs — the source debate documents processed so far.

Speakers — every MP with a statement in the dataset, each with a profile page.

Sessions — an overview of Lok Sabha sessions and their sitting dates.

Where this comes from

Source PDFs are published by the Lok Sabha Secretariat at eparlib.sansad.in. Statements are extracted and attributed automatically, non-English statements are translated where noted, and daily summaries are AI-generated from the parsed statements — treat them as a starting point for finding the primary source, not a substitute for it.
