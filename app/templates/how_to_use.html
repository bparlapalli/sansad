How to use ParamaSrota

A searchable record of what was actually said on the floor of the Lok Sabha

ParamaSrota parses Lok Sabha debate PDFs into individual, attributed statements — who said what, on which date, on which subject — and makes them searchable instead of leaving them buried in a scanned transcript.

Search modes

By Date — pick a sitting day and see everything said that day, in order.

Politician — pull up every statement from a specific MP, optionally combined with a keyword.

Party — see all MPs from a party and what they've said, optionally filtered by date or keyword.

Free Text — search across every statement for a word or phrase (a bill name, a scheme, a topic).

Reading a result

A speech in the House can run to hundreds of words covering several subjects. When you search by keyword, each result shows the specific passage that matched — not the whole speech — so you're not hunting through a wall of text to find why it came up.

Click "Show full statement" under a result to expand it to the complete remarks, with full speaker, date, and page context.

What's in the data right now

This is an early, actively-growing dataset. It currently covers recent sitting days rather than the full parliamentary record — older sessions are being added over time. If a date or debate you're looking for isn't here yet, it's likely still in the pipeline.

Other pages

Today — the latest sitting day's summary and proceedings.

News — a running briefing of recent parliamentary activity.

PDFs — the source debate documents that have been processed so far.

Speakers — every MP with a statement in the dataset, with a profile page each.

Sessions — an overview of Lok Sabha sessions and their sitting dates.

Where this comes from

Source PDFs are published by the Lok Sabha Secretariat at eparlib.sansad.in. Statements are extracted and attributed automatically, non-English statements are translated where noted, and daily summaries are AI-generated from the parsed statements — treat them as a starting point for finding the primary source, not a substitute for it.
